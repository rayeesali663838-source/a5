Miso soup with tofu & wakame

Serves 415 minHome kitchen

Miso soup is eaten with almost every Japanese meal, from breakfast onwards. The secret is simple: never let the soup boil once the miso goes in, or it loses its aroma.

Ingredients

  • 800 ml dashi (classic or kombu & shiitake)
  • 3–4 tbsp miso paste
  • 200 g silken or firm tofu, cubed
  • 1 tbsp dried wakame seaweed
  • 2 spring onions, finely sliced

Method

  1. Soak the wakame in water for 5 minutes, then drain.
  2. Bring the dashi to a gentle simmer and add the tofu to warm through.
  3. Turn off the heat. Dissolve the miso in a ladle of dashi, then stir it into the pot.
  4. Add the wakame and spring onion and serve straight away.

Kitchen note: White miso is mild and sweet; red miso is deeper and saltier. Mixing the two gives a balanced, everyday flavour.

Cold soba with dipping sauce

Serves 220 minHome kitchen

On hot days, zaru soba is a favourite: chilled noodles dipped into a cool, savoury sauce. It’s light, refreshing and quick to put together.

Ingredients

  • 200 g dried soba noodles
  • 250 ml dashi
  • 4 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • Sliced spring onion, shredded nori and grated fresh wasabi to serve

Method

  1. Simmer the dashi, soy sauce and sugar for 2 minutes, then cool and chill.
  2. Cook the soba according to the packet, usually 4–5 minutes.
  3. Drain and rinse under cold running water, rubbing gently to remove starch.
  4. Serve on a plate or bamboo tray with the dip in small bowls and the toppings on the side.

Kitchen note: Look for soba with a high buckwheat content for a nuttier flavour. Pure buckwheat soba is naturally wheat-free, but check the label.

Hiyayakko chilled tofu

Serves 25 minHome kitchen

This is the simplest dish in the Japanese home repertoire: cold silken tofu with a few bright toppings. It’s cooling in summer and a perfect side for any meal.

Ingredients

  • 300 g silken tofu, chilled
  • 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
  • 1 spring onion, finely sliced
  • Toasted sesame seeds
  • Soy sauce to drizzle

Method

  1. Drain the tofu gently and pat dry with kitchen paper.
  2. Cut into two or four blocks and place in small bowls.
  3. Top with ginger, spring onion and sesame seeds.
  4. Drizzle with soy sauce just before eating.

Kitchen note: Try a spoonful of finely chopped pickled cucumber or a little grated yuzu zest for variety.

Seared salmon rice bowl with wasabi

Serves 225 minHome kitchen

A comforting bowl of warm rice topped with crisp-skinned salmon, a quick soy glaze and a little fresh wasabi. A weeknight favourite that feels special.

Ingredients

  • 2 salmon fillets, skin on
  • 2 bowls of freshly cooked short-grain rice
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp rice vinegar and 1 tsp sugar
  • Fresh wasabi, nori strips and sesame to serve

Method

  1. Pat the salmon dry and season lightly with salt.
  2. Cook skin-side down in a hot, lightly oiled pan for 5 minutes until crisp, then turn and cook for 2–3 minutes more until cooked through.
  3. Add the soy sauce, vinegar and sugar to the pan and spoon over the fish as it bubbles.
  4. Flake the salmon over the rice, drizzle with the glaze and finish with wasabi, nori and sesame.

Kitchen note: Cook salmon until it flakes easily and reaches 63°C (145°F) in the thickest part.

Agedashi tofu

Serves 2–325 minHome kitchen

Crisp on the outside and soft within, agedashi tofu sits in a warm, savoury broth that slowly softens the coating. It’s a classic izakaya-style dish that is easy to make at home.

Ingredients

  • 400 g firm or medium tofu
  • 4 tbsp potato starch or cornflour
  • Oil for shallow-frying
  • 200 ml dashi, 2 tbsp soy sauce and 1 tsp sugar
  • Grated daikon, ginger and spring onion to serve

Method

  1. Wrap the tofu in kitchen paper and press under a plate for 15 minutes.
  2. Cut into large cubes and coat lightly in starch.
  3. Shallow-fry in 1 cm of oil until golden and crisp on all sides; drain.
  4. Warm the dashi, soy sauce and sugar, pour into bowls, add the tofu and top with daikon, ginger and spring onion.

Kitchen note: Coat the tofu just before frying so the starch stays dry and crisp.

Vegetable tempura

Serves 440 minHome kitchen

Good tempura is light and lacy, never heavy. The trick is ice-cold batter, mixed only briefly so it stays lumpy, and frying in small batches.

Ingredients

  • 100 g plain flour (plus extra for dusting)
  • 200 ml ice-cold sparkling water
  • Sweet potato, aubergine, green beans, shiitake and pepper, sliced
  • Vegetable oil for deep-frying
  • Dashi, soy sauce and grated daikon for dipping

Method

  1. Heat the oil to 170–175°C (340–350°F) in a deep, heavy pan.
  2. Dust the vegetables with flour so the batter clings.
  3. Stir the flour into the ice-cold water with chopsticks for a few seconds only; lumps are fine.
  4. Dip and fry a few pieces at a time for 2–3 minutes until crisp, then drain on a rack and serve at once.

Kitchen note: Never leave hot oil unattended, keep a lid nearby and let the oil cool completely before disposing of it.

Rice first

Start the rice before anything else. It needs soaking, cooking and resting, which gives you time to make soup and sides.

Season gently

Japanese home food is lightly seasoned. Taste as you go and let the dashi and vegetables lead.

Allergens

Recipes may contain soy, wheat, fish, sesame and egg. Check labels, especially soy sauce and noodles, and adapt to your needs.

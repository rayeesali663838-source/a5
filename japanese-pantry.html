The Japanese pantry, explained

A short list of staples unlocks most everyday Japanese cooking. Here’s what to buy, how to store it and how we use it.

Start here

Five staples cover most meals

If you’re new to Japanese cooking, begin with short-grain rice, soy sauce, rice vinegar, miso and kombu. With these you can make rice, miso soup, dipping sauces, quick pickles and a simple dashi.

Next, add dried shiitake, sesame seeds, nori, potato starch and a good fresh ginger root. Everything else can come as you explore.

Miso

Fermented soybean paste in many styles. White (shiro) is mild and sweet, red (aka) is bold and salty, and mixed (awase) sits in between. Keep it in the fridge.

Wasabi

Buy fresh rhizome when you can and grate it to order. For tubes, read the label: many are mostly horseradish.

Tsukemono pickles

Quick salt or rice-vinegar pickles add crunch and brightness to rice. Cucumber, daikon and cabbage are easy places to start.

Matcha

Finely ground green tea for whisking and baking. Store it airtight in the fridge and use within a couple of months of opening.

Yuzu

A fragrant citrus whose zest and juice brighten dressings, ponzu and soups. Bottled juice works well when fresh fruit is hard to find.

A good knife

A sharp, thin-bladed knife makes fine slicing of vegetables and tofu much easier. Hand-wash, dry straight away and hone often.

Quick reference

Staples at a glance

StapleWhat it doesHow to store
Short-grain riceSticky, tender grains that hold together for bowls and onigiriAirtight, cool and dry; use within a few months
Soy sauceSalt and savoury depth; light soy is saltier and paler, dark soy is richerFridge after opening for the best flavour
Rice vinegarGentle acidity for sushi rice, pickles and dressingsCupboard, tightly closed
KombuDried kelp that gives dashi its savoury baseAirtight in the cupboard; the white powder on it is flavour, so don’t wash it off
Dried shiitakeRich, earthy flavour for vegan dashi and simmered dishesAirtight, away from light
NoriToasted seaweed sheets for rice balls and garnishSealed with a desiccant; re-crisp briefly over a flame or in a dry pan
Potato starchLight, crisp coatings and silky thickened saucesAirtight in the cupboard

Read labels

Soy sauce and many noodles contain wheat. Tamari is often wheat-free, but always check.

Salt matters

Miso, soy and pickles are salty. Balance them with plenty of rice, vegetables and unsalted sides.

Buy small

Matcha, sesame and nori lose freshness quickly, so buy small packs and use them often.
